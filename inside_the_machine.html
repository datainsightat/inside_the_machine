Before entry · Important calibration

This is a visual metaphor, not a literal view of a mind. AI does not experience the world, possess feelings, or form personal desires. It receives information, detects patterns, evaluates possible outputs, and acts within instructions and constraints.

Prologue · AI in plain language

Before the machine:
what is AI?

AI is a broad name for computer systems designed to perform tasks that normally involve human intelligence—such as recognizing patterns, working with language, or making predictions.

The short versionAI is not magic and it is not one single machine. It is software built with mathematical methods and examples. Different AI systems are built for different jobs.
01 · LEARN

Patterns from examples

During training, an AI system processes many examples and adjusts internal numbers so it becomes better at a task. It learns statistical patterns—not a list of hand-written answers.

02 · APPLY

Input becomes output

After training, the system receives an input and uses those learned patterns to produce an output: a prediction, recommendation, image, classification, or piece of language.

03 · GENERATE

Language models

This journey focuses on a language model: a type of generative AI trained to continue and transform language. Its responses can be useful and fluent without being conscious or always correct.

    Myths vs. reality · Open each signal

    “AI understands exactly like a human.”

    Reality: language models learn numerical patterns in data. Their capabilities can resemble understanding, but their mechanisms and relationship to the world differ profoundly from human experience.

    “AI always knows when it is wrong.”

    Reality: models can generate incorrect claims without reliably detecting them. Calibrated uncertainty and external verification are essential.

    “AI has its own hidden personal goals.”

    Reality: deployed systems operate through learned behavior, instructions, and designed objectives—not personal wants or intentions.

    “A fluent answer must be factual.”

    Reality: fluency measures how well language patterns fit. Truth may require evidence, calculation, current data, or trusted sources.

    “AI remembers everything.”

    Reality: the system primarily works from its available context and any explicitly connected memory mechanisms. Context can be incomplete or limited.

    “AI and humans perceive the same world.”

    Reality: humans have bodies, senses, lived histories, and subjective awareness. A language model receives encoded inputs and produces outputs through computation.

    An AI response can look like a window into a mind, but it is better understood as the output of a complex system shaped by data, context, computation, instructions, and human design.
